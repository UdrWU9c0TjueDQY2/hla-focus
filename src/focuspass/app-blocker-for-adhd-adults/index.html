Questions ADHD adults ask first

What is the best app blocker for adults with ADHD?

The one you do not have to argue with at the moment of weakest attention. For ADHD that usually means: a native shield rather than a nag, a short deliberate way through it (a Pass with a reason), an automatic relock so you never have to remember to re-block, and no streak to lose. FocusPass is built that way, and we make it, so weigh that; the seven days above are the honest test.

Why do I bypass every blocker I install?

Because most of them put the decision on you, repeatedly, and ADHD makes repeated small decisions expensive. A shield that offers Go Back or a short Pass replaces the argument with two buttons. The relock is scheduled with iOS before the app opens, so protection resumes even if FocusPass is closed.

Does FocusPass use Screen Time?

Yes. You pick apps, categories or websites with Apple's own Screen Time picker, and the shield is a native one. Opening a protected app shows PROTECTED, with Go Back or Use a Pass.

Will it shame me with streaks?

No. Every Go Back is a Focus Save, counted honestly, and the optional progress is a calm momentum system and missions. Nothing resets to zero because you had a bad Tuesday.

Three ways to block an app

Delete it

Works for 36 hours. Reinstalling takes nine seconds and the shame costs more than the scroll did.

Hard lock

No way through at all. Fine until the one real reason, at which point the whole thing gets switched off and never switched on again.

Shield with a Pass

A native shield, a short deliberate way through with a reason, an automatic relock, and a count of every Go Back. The decision happens once, at setup. That is what the seven days build.

The seven days

Day 1Why did the last blocker fail me in three days?

Because it asked you to decide, at the moment of weakest attention, whether to break a rule. ADHD brains are fine at rules and terrible at that moment. So on Day 1 do nothing clever: pick the two apps that eat the most time, shield them, and set five Passes a day. Five is generous on purpose. The point today is to meet the shield, not to win.

Day 2I used all five Passes by lunch. Is that failure?

No, it is data, and it is the data you came for. Each Pass had a reason you tapped (Reply, Check something, Research, or none). Look at the reasons tonight. Most ADHD adults find two Passes were real and three were a reflex. Tomorrow you set the Pass length to 5 minutes. Short Passes are the trick: long enough to reply, too short to disappear into.

Day 3What do I do when the shield comes up and I have no reason?

Tap Go Back. That is the whole move. FocusPass records it as a Focus Save, one honest count of a choice, with no invented minutes. You are not trying to feel disciplined; you are trying to make Go Back the fast, boring default. Count your Go Backs tonight. Three is a good Day 3.

Day 4I keep taking a Pass right after a Pass ends.

Turn on the Reset Window. It stops one Pass turning into three by holding a gap after each one. If you are out of Passes and genuinely need one thing, Task Access gives up to three minutes for a specific task; it never spends a Pass and it is limited so it cannot become a loop. That limit is the feature.

Day 5Mornings are fine. Evenings are a disaster.

That is the heatmap talking before you have even seen it. Set an Evening Zone: protected hours for the two apps from 8 pm. Essentials you must always reach stay available. If you work from home, a Work Zone for the afternoon slump is the other common one. Zones do the deciding so you do not have to at 9 pm.

Day 6Can I do a real focus block without feeling trapped?

Start a 25-minute Deep Focus from Home, Siri, the Watch or a widget. Protected apps stay shielded, the timer sits in the Dynamic Island, and Focus Rescue gives a short once-per-session exit for something truly essential. One exit, not a door. Most people do 25 the first time and 45 the second.

Day 7What did the week actually tell me?

Open the Pass heatmap. When there is enough data, FocusPass names one pattern ("Most of your Passes happen after 9 PM") and offers a fix you control. Weekly Review and Focus Saves by day are calculated on your device. Decide one change for next week, and only one. Then keep the shield. The week was never the goal; the default was.

Day 4, 9:40 pm

A composite scenario from people we have talked with, not a customer story.

Jo has deleted and reinstalled the same app eleven times this year. Tonight the shield comes up instead: PROTECTED, Go Back, Use a Pass. Jo takes a 5-minute Pass with the reason "reply", replies in ninety seconds, and sits looking at the remaining three and a half minutes with the specific, unfamiliar feeling of a Pass that is about to end on its own.

It ends. The app relocks. The Reset Window holds for a bit, and when the shield comes up again at 9:52 there is no reason to type, so Jo taps Go Back, and the app counts it: one Focus Save, no invented minutes, no guess about what Jo did next. What Jo did next was the dishes, which is not in the app and does not need to be.

By Day 7 the heatmap says most Passes happen after 9 pm, and offers an Evening Zone. Jo takes it. The eleven reinstalls were never about willpower. They were about a door that did not close by itself.
