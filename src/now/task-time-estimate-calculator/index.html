THE WORKED EXAMPLE

Three ratios.
One visible assumption.

Divide actual by estimated.

15 ÷ 10 = 1.50
25 ÷ 20 = 1.25
45 ÷ 30 = 1.50

Take the middle ratio.

Sort them: 1.25, 1.50, 1.50. The middle value is 1.50. This is not the ratio of the total minutes.

Try a planning scenario.

A new 20-minute estimate multiplied by 1.50 gives 30 minutes. That is ten more minutes in this example, not a promise about the next task.

A ratio below one means the task took less time than estimated. A ratio above one means it took more. Neither result measures effort, ability or how well you used the time.

What belongs in the comparison?

A familiar routine is different from learning a new process. If one task included a long interruption and the others did not, decide whether you are comparing focused work or real-world elapsed time. Do not silently change that definition to get a tidier number.

What the number can tell you.

Why use the median instead of an average?

This tool uses the middle of three ratios so its rule is easy to inspect. It is one descriptive choice, not a validated model for predicting your future work. A different summary can produce a different planning scenario.

Does this calculate a deadline?

No. It has no information about your calendar, breaks, priorities, travel or other commitments. The result is only an arithmetic example in minutes.

What about a task that took zero minutes?

This version accepts positive whole minutes only. Use completed tasks with an actual duration you can describe; it does not handle cancelled tasks or seconds.

Are the times saved?

No. Inputs stay on this page and are not submitted or saved by the tool. The copy button places the review on your clipboard when you choose it.
