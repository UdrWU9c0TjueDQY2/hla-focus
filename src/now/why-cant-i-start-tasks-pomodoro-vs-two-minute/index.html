Six myths about starting

  • MythYou just need more discipline.

    FactStarting is a skill with a name. CHADD describes executive functions that activate, organise and manage other functions, and says impairments affect the ability to begin, work on and complete tasks. A person who cannot start is not lazy; the activate step is the one that is stuck, and it responds to structure, not scolding.

    CHADD, Executive Function Skills
  • MythA longer to-do list will get you moving.

    FactA list is where tasks go to wait. The thing that moves you is one task with one concrete first step. Now’s screen shows exactly that, and its daily plan holds three tasks, not thirty; everything else waits in a parking lot where nothing is deleted.

    App Store listing, 4 Nov 2026
  • MythTwenty-five minutes is the right unit.

    FactFor continuing, maybe. For starting, it is a wall. The Pomodoro Technique is 25-minute blocks by design; the two-minute start is a different tool for a different problem, and the two are not rivals. Start with two, then pick 5, 10, 15 or 25.

    The Pomodoro Technique; listing
  • MythStopping early means you failed.

    FactEnding early still counts, in Now’s own words. A task touched for ninety seconds is a task that exists in your day; the one you never opened is not.

    App Store listing
  • MythBreaking a task down is procrastination.

    FactOnly if it never ends. One first step, small enough to be slightly embarrassing, is the opposite of procrastination. Put five dishes in the sink is a real instruction; clean the kitchen is a mood.

    Listing example
  • MythAn ADHD app treats ADHD.

    FactNo. Now’s listing says it is a productivity-support tool that does not diagnose, treat or manage ADHD or any medical condition. If starting is impossible most days, that is a conversation with a clinician, not a download.

Questions people ask next

Why can’t I start tasks even when I want to?

Because starting is its own step, separate from wanting. CHADD’s description of executive function includes activating for tasks as one of the clusters that can be impaired; when it is, the gap between “I should” and “I am” does not close on its own. What closes it is a smaller first step, a visible short timer, and permission to stop. That is the entire design of the two-minute start.

Pomodoro or the two-minute rule?

Both, in order. The two-minute start gets you over the activation gap; a Pomodoro-length block (25 minutes) is for staying once you are in. Now does the first by default and offers 5, 10, 15 or 25 minutes, custom, or no timer after it.

What is body doubling?

Working alongside another person, in a room or on a call, so that their presence makes starting easier. It is widely used in ADHD communities; it needs another human, which is its strength and its limit. No app replaces it; Now’s Mochi companion naps while you focus and is explicitly optional and never needy.
