Honest limits

Three ways parents handle the fog

Push through

Lists on the fridge, coffee, self-blame. The lists help. The blame does not, and it is the part that sticks.

Streak apps

A brain app that counts days in a row is a brain app that will shame you by Thursday. The streak breaks on the first bad night, and then so does the habit.

Six minutes on the days with a nap, no timer, ratings that need several sessions to move. Rhythm counts days per week; Grace covers the bad ones. That is how Cognilo is built, and the free tier is the whole of it.

Questions new parents ask

Is it normal to be forgetful after having a baby?

Common enough to have a nickname. Broken sleep, a brain running two people's schedules, and hormonal change all pull on the same working memory. Most parents describe it easing as sleep returns. If it does not ease, or it comes with low mood, numbness or thoughts that frighten you, talk to your GP or midwife; that is a different thing and it is treatable. This is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your clinician.

Will brain games fix baby brain?

No, and Cognilo's own listing says its ratings describe how you play its puzzles, not intelligence or health. What a short untimed session can do is give you a measurable, private marker that the lapses are temporary, and a few techniques (the Mastery tracks) for holding a list in your head when you have to. Sleep is the treatment. This is the company.

Then you do not, and the app is built for that: Rhythm counts days per week rather than an unbroken streak, Grace and Vacation cover the weeks that fall apart, and nothing nags. Play on the days with a nap. Skip the rest without guilt.
