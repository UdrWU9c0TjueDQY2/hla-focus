A worked example

For 3, 7, 11, 15, the displayed differences are +4, +4 and +4. Among the offered rules, adding 4 fits each step and predicts 19, 23.

Check differences first

Subtract each term from the next. If the difference is constant, a repeated addition or subtraction rule may fit.

Then test multiplication or alternation

A constant ratio can suggest multiplication. Alternating differences can suggest two steps that repeat in order.

Do not claim a finite list proves a unique rule

Many constructed rules can fit a short list. Each puzzle here has one intended rule among its displayed choices, not the only mathematically possible continuation.

Questions before you continue

Can more than one rule fit a number sequence?

Yes. A finite list can have many continuations. This activity asks you to choose among three stated rules.

What happens if I pick the wrong rule?

You get a prompt to check every transition and can try again or reveal the explanation.

Is there a timer or score?

No. Work at your own pace and use the explanation whenever you want.
