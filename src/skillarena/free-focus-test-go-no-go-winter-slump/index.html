The afternoon slump has a reputation for being a character flaw, and it is mostly a clock. NIOSH's own training on long work hours describes the early afternoon as a time when the circadian rhythms that promote wakefulness dip while the pressure to sleep keeps building. In December the morning light that would prop that rhythm up arrives late and leaves early. So you sit at your desk at 3:10 pm, read the same sentence three times, and conclude that you are bad at your job. You are not. You are in a trough.

What a free focus test gives you is a number for the trough. Not a diagnosis, not a rating of your brain, just a way to see that your reaction time at 3 pm is slower than at 10 am and that you tapped on two red squares you would have left alone this morning. That is useful for one reason: it turns a vague feeling into something you can act on, which usually means standing up, going outside into whatever light there is, and coming back.

The slump is not a character flaw. It is a clock, and in December the clock runs on less light.

Questions people ask the test

What is a go/no-go test?

A simple attention task: a signal appears, you respond to most of them (go) and withhold the response to a rarer one (no-go). It measures two things at once: whether you respond fast enough when you should, and whether you can stop yourself when you should not. The lab version is the Sustained Attention to Response Task (Robertson et al., 1997); the one above is a 24-trial browser sketch of the idea, and SkillArena's Deep Focus is a 30-trial version.

Is this free focus test accurate?

Accurate enough to notice a slump, not accurate enough to diagnose anything. Browser timing, screen refresh and the finger you use all add noise. Run it twice at 10 am and twice at 3 pm and compare your own numbers; comparing yourself with a table of other people is where it stops being useful.

Why do I lose focus around 3 pm?

Partly biology. NIOSH's training materials on work hours describe an afternoon period when the circadian rhythms that promote wakefulness dip while sleep pressure keeps building. Short daylight in December removes the morning light that props the rhythm up, which is one reason the slump feels worse now than in June. The fix is not an app; it is light, movement and sleep. The app is a four-minute way to notice.

What does SkillArena actually claim?

Per its listing: it is a game, ratings describe your performance in SkillArena's own games, and they are not an IQ score or a medical, psychological or clinical assessment. We like that sentence and we are not going to contradict it here.

Is SkillArena free?

Yes. The listing describes it as a free skill game with no account and no ads, all games working offline; optional Game Center leaderboards need a connection. There is no subscription mentioned in the listing.

How long is a session?

The Daily Arena is four games in about four minutes; Quick is about two and Extended about eight. Timers pause if you leave the app mid-round, per the release notes.
