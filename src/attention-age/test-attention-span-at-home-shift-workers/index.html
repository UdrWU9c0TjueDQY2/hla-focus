A typical bad week, made up but familiar

Fourth night shift in a row. Home at 8:10, asleep by 9:30, awake at 1:40 because the courier rang. Four hours, maybe. The plan was to run the five-minute test at 3 pm. On a week like this, the honest move is to run it and expect a lower number. The result is not "my attention is bad"; it is "my attention on four hours of daytime sleep is bad", which is a different fact, and a useful one if you keep it next to the rested weeks.

That is the whole point of testing at home rather than once in a clinic: you can collect the tired results and the rested ones, and see how far apart they sit.

Who should pick something else

Anyone looking for a diagnosis

The Attention Index is not a clinical measure and the app does not claim otherwise. If you are worried about ADHD, concussion or a sleep disorder, a clinician can run validated tests. This app is for practice and tracking.

Questions tired people ask

How do I test my attention span at home?

Use a timed task with a fixed scoring rule and repeat it under similar conditions. Online quizzes that ask how you feel measure mood, not attention. Attention Age runs three timed tasks on your iPhone in about five minutes, scores them on a 0 to 100 index with five category scores, and keeps every result so you can compare week to week.

Will a bad night's sleep change my score?

Expect it to. Short-term sleep loss has its largest measured effect on sustained attention and vigilance (Lim and Dinges, 2010 meta-analysis). That is why this page starts with a sleep check: a score taken after four hours of sleep tells you about that night, not about your attention in general.

How many hours of sleep count as enough?

The CDC's recommendation for adults aged 18 to 60 is seven or more hours per night. Shift workers often get less on work days and sleep in daylight, which is lighter and more broken, so a weekly average is a better guide than any single night.

Is the Attention Index a medical score or an ADHD test?

No. The app says so itself: it is an entertainment and self-practice app, the index reflects performance on these specific tasks on this device, and it is not an age, a percentile, a ranking or a diagnosis. If attention problems are affecting work or safety, talk to a clinician.

Why does the app call a 3-point change 'about the same'?

Because small day-to-day swings are mostly noise: sleep, caffeine, mood, how carefully you tapped. Attention Age treats changes under 5 points as 'about the same' and flags early gains that probably come from learning the tasks rather than from better attention.

Does the app need an account or send my data anywhere?

No account, no ads, no third-party analytics, per the listing. Results stay on the iPhone; you can export them as CSV or delete everything from Settings. Deep Work sessions can optionally be written to Apple Health as Mindful Minutes.
