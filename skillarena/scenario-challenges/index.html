Fixed first tile case 1

Three different tiles A, B and C must form a row, using every tile exactly once. The first tile is required to be A. How many different complete rows remain possible? A row and its reversal count separately when both are allowed.

Show the worked answer

C. 2

Fixing A leaves two unused tiles and two spaces. Either remaining tile can occupy the second space, after which the last is forced. Therefore exactly two complete rows satisfy the first-position restriction.
