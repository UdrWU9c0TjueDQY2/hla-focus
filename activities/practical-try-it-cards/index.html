Start, notice, compare

Try one concrete task, record what happened and compare the result with the rule. These exercises do not diagnose attention or measure intelligence. There is no score to compare against other people.

These publisher-created companion activities are available here without installing anything. Work on your own paper; this page collects no answers.

Independent companion activity · SkillArena: Focus Training

Estimate, then count a grouped collection

Independent paper game for adults or an adult-led group. Draw four rows containing 3, 5, 4 and 6 dots. Look briefly and write an estimate of the total before counting carefully. The exact total is 18. Compare the estimate with 18 using absolute difference: an estimate of 16 is two away, and 21 is three away. Now explain whether grouping rows would help a second attempt. Do not call either result an ability score; it describes this one drawing and attempt. This activity is separate from SkillArena's native estimate games. The app offers a different collection of short rounds for players who enjoy the task.

Independent companion activity · Attention Age: Focus Test

A fair-comparison card for two attempts

Independent recreational record exercise. Draw two columns labeled Attempt A and Attempt B, with rows for task, rule, device, interruption and result. Fill a fictional example: the same arrow task on the same phone, with results 16 and 18 correct out of 20; note that B happened after practicing the rule. The arithmetic difference is two correct answers, but that alone does not establish broader improvement. Ask which conditions changed and what remains unknown. Keep the record private and use invented details for any public example. Attention Age has its own task history; this card explains comparison limits rather than copying or diagnosing from its score.

Independent companion activity · Cognilo: Brain Training Games

A mental sum with an explained route

Independent paper puzzle. Work out 48 + 27 without a calculator, then explain the route rather than just announcing the result. One method is 48 + 20 = 68, followed by 68 + 7 = 75. Another is 50 + 27 = 77, then subtracting the extra 2 to reach 75. Both are valid. Compare which felt easier for this particular sum; neither method is required for every person or problem. This is an original recreational example, not a Cognilo level or assessment. The app offers separate number rounds within its Daily 5 for players interested in continuing with varied puzzles.

Independent companion activity · Now: ADHD Task Starter

Shrink an outcome into a visible first action

Use the fictional task “Prepare the meeting report.” Write the outcome at the top of a page, then list three possible first actions: open the report file, write the meeting date, or gather the existing notes. Choose only one that can be physically started now. For this example, “Open the report file and type the date” is concrete; “Get organized” is not. Leave the remaining work visible but outside the first-action box. This independent exercise does not require completing the report. Now supports editable task breakdown and short starts; it does not automatically import this worksheet or guarantee that starting becomes easy.

Independent companion activity · FocusPass: App Blocker

Write an access intention with a finish line

Use a fictional task: open a messaging app to confirm a meeting location. Write three lines on paper: Purpose, Maximum planned window and Done when. Fill them with “Check the location,” “Five minutes for this example,” and “The address is noted privately.” A finish condition is more specific than “use the app less.” When the task is done, the user can choose to leave even if time remains. This independent planning exercise does not block anything or prescribe a universal limit. FocusPass provides native protected-app choices and timed access on a supported, properly configured device.

Independent companion activity · Time Debt: Screen Time

A two-day allowance worked example

Independent paper exercise: imagine a normal allowance of 40 minutes on each of two days. On day one, a fictional person borrows 10 additional minutes from day two. Write the two allowances as 50 and 30. Add them: 80 minutes, the same total as 40 plus 40. The example illustrates moving an allowance, not creating more time or predicting actual phone use. Now try borrowing 5 instead; the pair becomes 45 and 35. Time Debt provides a native borrowing preview for supported plans. Check the actual app's minimum and repayment settings before assuming this simple example matches a real plan.
